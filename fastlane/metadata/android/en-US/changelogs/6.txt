• LEDs. System · LEDs lists every LED the router exposes, with the modes its own triggers allow — board default, on, off, blink, heartbeat, network activity, Wi-Fi traffic — and works out which colours the LEDs behind one lens can make together. A connectivity watch can be installed from the phone: pick a colour for internet, upstream-only and no-upstream from that palette, preview it on the router, and a small procd service keeps the lens honest and survives upgrades.

• Mesh. Network · Mesh turns on seamless hand-off (802.11r/k/v) across your SSIDs and makes your other OpenWrt routers nodes of this one, over a cable or an 802.11s wireless link. A node keeps your Wi-Fi and leaves DHCP, DNS and the firewall to the primary; a backup is saved first, the node rolls itself back if the app cannot reach it again, and Leave mesh restores it.

• Two routers on the same address can both be saved. Pull down to refresh. Backup-screen crash fixes.

• Terminal keyboard: the symbol layer has a second page (=\<) with backslash, brackets, braces, underscore, comma, backtick, caret and percent.

• Far less load on the router. Reading the radios, the client list and the dashboard tick now use iw, netifd and rpcd instead of the iwinfo binary, which cost up to a second of CPU per call on MIPS routers: connecting went from ~10 s at 100% CPU to under a second, and the 1 Hz dashboard tick from 0.5 s to 0.1 s of CPU.
